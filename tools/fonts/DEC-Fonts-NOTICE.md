## DEC VT220 glyph source

`rom-separated.png` is an unmodified copy of the ROM-representation image
published by Paul Flo Williams at
<https://www.vt100.net/dec/vt220/rom-separated.png> (SHA-256
`0ab9b7f8650f143efc74f8ab81616ef10ff8b565976ed89162f05cd3f6766a69`).
VT100.net states that the image was produced by dumping a 1986-vintage
VT220-F3 with version 2.3 firmware, DEC part `23-178E6`; see
<https://www.vt100.net/dec/vt220/glyphs.html>. The source page did not state an
explicit redistribution license for the image or linked firmware when checked
on 2026-07-19.

The following tracked materials contain, modify, or display the historical
glyph data:

- `rom-separated.png`
- `rom-separated.xcf`
- `rom-separated_extended.png`
- `rom-separated-extended.xcf`
- `dist/fonts/`
- `sample.png`
- `nethack_DECgraphics.png`

The MIT grant applies to Taylor Hardy's copyrightable contributions to these
files. It does not purport to relicense the underlying historical DEC glyph

## Jurisdictional context

In the United States,
[37 C.F.R. section 202.1(e)](https://www.copyright.gov/title37/202/37cfr202-1.html)
lists "typeface as typeface" among material not subject to copyright. That
rule does not by itself determine rights in font software or data, firmware, a
source image, trademarks, or uses governed by another jurisdiction.
[WIPO's survey analysis](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/mdocs/en/sct_37/sct_37_2_rev.pdf)
reports that typeface and type-font designs may receive protection under
different bodies of law in many responding jurisdictions. This paragraph is
informational and is not legal advice or a warranty.
